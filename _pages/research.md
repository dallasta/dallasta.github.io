## Research Overview

My research lies at the interface between **statistical physics, complex systems, and theoretical approaches to inference and dynamics**. I develop analytical and computational methods to understand collective phenomena in high-dimensional interacting systems, with particular attention to disorder, network structure, stochastic dynamics, and non-equilibrium processes.

A recurring theme of my work is the use of concepts and techniques from statistical mechanics to address problems in which the collective behaviour of many interacting components gives rise to emergent phenomena that cannot be understood from the properties of the individual elements alone.

## Research Perspective

Across these themes, I am interested in identifying **general principles and mathematical structures underlying complex collective behaviour**. Statistical mechanics provides a natural framework for this purpose, allowing methods developed for physical systems to be adapted to problems in networks, inference, spreading processes, and other complex systems.

My research therefore combines theoretical analysis, probabilistic methods, and computational approaches, with the aim of developing models and algorithms that are both mathematically tractable and relevant to real-world complex systems.
