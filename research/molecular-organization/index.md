Biological systems achieve remarkable levels of organization through the collective behaviour of many interacting molecular components. My research in this area uses **statistical physics to understand how molecular interactions, phase separation, and non-equilibrium processes generate spatial organization in biological systems**.

A particular focus is the organization of molecular species on cell membranes and the emergence of compartments through phase separation and related mechanisms.

<br>

## Molecular Sorting

Cells must selectively organize many molecular species in space and time. I study theoretical models of molecular sorting in which interactions, diffusion, and collective effects determine the spatial distribution of molecular components.

An important question is how biological systems can achieve efficient sorting through self-organization, without requiring an externally imposed spatial structure.

### Selected publications

- D. Andreghetti, L. Dall’Asta & A. Gamba, **Molecular sorting on a fluctuating membrane**, *SciPost Physics* (2025).
- A. Piras, E. Floris, L. Dall’Asta & A. Gamba, **Sorting of multiple molecular species on cell membranes**, *Physical Review E* (2023).
- M. Zamparo, D. Valdembri, G. Serini et al., **Optimality in Self-Organized Molecular Sorting**, *Physical Review Letters* (2021).

<br>

## Phase Separation & Criticality

Phase separation is a fundamental mechanism for the formation of spatially organized compartments in biological systems. I investigate how phase separation emerges from molecular interactions and how finite system size, geometry, and coupling between processes affect the resulting structures.

### Selected publications

- E. Floris, A. Piras, F. S. Pezzicoli, M. Zamparo, L. Dall’Asta & A. Gamba, **Phase separation and critical size in molecular sorting**, *Physical Review E* (2022).
- E. Floris, A. Piras, L. Dall’Asta, A. Gamba, E. Hirsch & C. C. Campa, **Physics of compartmentalization: How phase separation and signaling shape membrane and organelle identity** (2021).

<br>

## Membrane Organization

Biological membranes are fluctuating, heterogeneous environments in which molecular interactions can produce collective spatial structures.

My work explores how membrane fluctuations and molecular interactions influence sorting, organization, and the emergence of spatially structured phases.

### Selected publications

- D. Andreghetti, L. Dall’Asta, A. Gamba, I. Kolokolov & V. Lebedev, **Molecular sorting on a fluctuating membrane**, *SciPost Physics* (2025).
- A. Piras, E. Floris, L. Dall’Asta & A. Gamba, **Sorting of multiple molecular species on cell membranes**, *Physical Review E* (2023).
- E. Floris, A. Piras, F. S. Pezzicoli, M. Zamparo, L. Dall’Asta & A. Gamba, **Phase separation and critical size in molecular sorting**, *Physical Review E* (2022).

<br>

## Reaction-Driven Organization

Molecular organization can also be driven by chemical reactions and active processes rather than by passive equilibrium alone. This raises fundamental questions about the interplay between reaction kinetics, phase separation, and spatial organization.

Recent work investigates how enzymatic activity can drive and control phase separation, providing a connection between non-equilibrium statistical physics and biological organization.

### Selected publications

- D. Andreghetti, L. Dall’Asta & A. Gamba, **Enzyme-Driven Phase Separation**, *PRX Life* (2026).
- D. Andreghetti, L. Dall’Asta, A. Gamba, I. Kolokolov & V. Lebedev, **Molecular sorting on a fluctuating membrane**, *SciPost Physics* (2025).
